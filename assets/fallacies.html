The Fallacy Case File

A workshop dossier of the errors arguments make — how to name them, spot them, and teach them.
A logical fallacy is a flaw in reasoning — the conclusion doesn't actually follow from the premises, even if the argument sounds persuasive. Fallacies split into two families: formal fallacies break the argument's structure itself (they'd be invalid no matter the topic), and informal fallacies break down because of content, context, or delivery — irrelevant points, unearned assumptions, or slippery language.
